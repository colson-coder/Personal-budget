Built for people with more than one paycheck.

Side hustle, freelance work, a second currency? Most trackers assume one salary in one country. This one doesn't.

  • Any currency. Every entry converts at its own day's rate and totals show in your home currency.
  • Side income in a tap. Log a gig payment and set part of it aside right away.
  • Taxes for any country. Use one rate or your own brackets, with a tax year starting in any month.

No surprise tax bill.

Mark which income already has tax taken out and which doesn't. The app estimates what you'll owe and tells you how much to set aside each month.

  • A full-year forecast based on how you're earning so far
  • Status for each quarter for estimated payments
  • Deductible expenses are taken off automatically

Install it like an app on iPhone, Android, Mac or Windows, with no app store needed. With an account, everything syncs between your devices.

  • iPhone: open in Safari, then Share → Add to Home Screen
  • Mac: in Safari, choose File → Add to Dock
  • Android or Windows: choose Install app in the browser menu

Questions

Does it connect to my bank?

Not directly yet. Download a CSV or OFX file from your bank's website and import it. The app reads most formats, spots duplicates and learns your categories. On iPhone you can also set up a Shortcut that logs Apple Pay purchases as you make them.

Is my data private?

Without an account, everything stays in your browser on your device. With an account, your data is stored so that only you can read it. Nothing is sold or shared.

Does it work outside the US?

Yes. Choose any home currency and timezone, log entries in other currencies, and set the tax rules for where you live.

Is this tax or financial advice?

No. Tax figures are estimates to help you plan. Check with a qualified professional before you file.

What does it cost?

It's free during early access. If paid extras are added later, the core tracker will stay free.

Why "Babylon's Purse"?

It's named after the classic parable The Richest Man in Babylon, whose first rule is to keep a part of everything you earn. The money plan follows that rule.
